Warning Signs

Signs you need a pest control visit

Pests are good at hiding. These are the signs that mean it is time to call.

You see bugs in daylight

Roaches and mice hide by day. Seeing them in daylight often means their hiding spots are full.

Droppings in drawers or the attic

Small dark specks point to roaches. Rice-sized pellets point to mice. Larger ones point to rats.

Noises in walls or ceilings

Scratching at night usually means rats. Daytime running often means squirrels.

Mud tubes or piles of wings

Mud tubes on the slab and wings near windows are classic termite signs.

Bites you cannot explain

Bites in lines after sleeping point to bed bugs. Bites on ankles point to fleas.

New mounds or nests

Fire ant mounds after rain, or wasp nests under the eaves, should be handled before someone gets stung.

Local Pest Guide

Common pests in Pensacola and why they thrive here

Pensacola's heat, humidity and water bring a steady mix of pests. These are the ones homeowners call about most.

Termites

Native subterranean and Formosan termites live in the soil and build mud tubes into homes. Formosan colonies can hold millions of termites and swarm on warm evenings in late spring, often near lights. Drywood termites also turn up in attic framing and furniture. See termite treatment.

Palmetto bugs and German roaches

Large American and smoky brown roaches, called palmetto bugs, live in mulch, palms and drains and fly to lights on summer nights. Small German roaches breed indoors in kitchens and spread through apartments. See cockroach control.

Mosquitoes

Rain, Pensacola Bay, Escambia Bay and bayous like Texar and Chico give mosquitoes endless places to breed. Asian tiger mosquitoes bite during the day and breed in small containers. See mosquito control.

Roof rats and mice

Roof rats climb trees, palms and power lines into attics. House mice slip in through garages and gaps at ground level. Calls rise in fall and after storms. See rodent control.

Fire ants

Red imported fire ants build mounds in sunny lawns after rain and sting in groups. They also pack AC units and electrical boxes. See fire ant control.

Bed bugs

Pensacola is a travel town, with beach visitors, military moves and college students. Bed bugs ride home in luggage and spread through shared walls. See bed bug treatment.

Spiders

Southern black widows and brown widows hide under patio furniture, in meter boxes and around pool pumps. Golden silk orb weavers string big webs in the trees in late summer. See spider control.

Wasps and yellowjackets

Paper wasps build under eaves, railings and grills. Yellowjackets nest in the ground and grow largest in late summer and fall. See wasp and hornet removal.

Fleas and ticks

Warm, humid weather lets fleas breed almost all year. Ticks wait along wooded yard edges in Pace, Ensley and Gulf Breeze. See flea control and tick control.

Good to Know

Do-it-yourself or call a pro?

Not every bug needs a professional. Here is how to tell the difference.

When DIY can work

A few ants on the counter, a single wasp nest you can reach safely, or a spider web on the porch. Store baits and good cleaning habits can handle small, simple problems.

When to call a pro

Termites, bed bugs, German roaches, rats in the attic, fire ants across the yard, yellowjacket ground nests and any wildlife. These need training, the right products and often a Florida license.

Why store sprays often fail

Sprays kill the bugs they touch but miss nests, eggs and hidden colonies. Some, like many ant and roach sprays, can scatter pests deeper into walls and make the problem harder to fix.

What a pro brings

A technician identifies the pest, finds where it gets in, treats the source and follows up. You also get a written price and, with most plans, free re-treatment if pests return.

Termite Protection

Termite treatment and inspections in Pensacola

Florida is one of the hardest-hit states for termites, and the Gulf Coast is a known hot spot for the Formosan subterranean termite. Escambia County has warned homeowners about Formosan termites for years, and University of Florida research shows they are still spreading. Formosan termites are blamed for about $1 billion in damage each year in the US.

Most homeowners insurance does not cover termite damage, so catching termites early matters. Watch for mud tubes on the slab, swarmers or piles of wings near windows, hollow-sounding wood and small piles of pellets.

Storm Season

Pests after a hurricane or flood in Pensacola

Pensacola knows hurricanes. Frederic, Ivan, Dennis and Sally all hit at or near the city, and the 2012 and 2014 floods soaked many neighborhoods. Storms do not just damage roofs. They change where pests live.

Floodwater drives fire ants, rodents and roaches out of the ground and into homes. Torn soffits and loose vents open new paths for roof rats and squirrels. Standing water left behind breeds mosquitoes within days. Wet wood draws termites and carpenter ants for months after the water is gone.

After-storm pest checklist

  • Dump standing water from buckets, toys, tarps and gutters within a few days
  • Check soffits, roof vents and the roof line for new gaps
  • Watch for fire ant rafts and new mounds near doors and play areas
  • Dry out wet wood and replace soaked insulation
  • Book a termite inspection if wood stayed wet for days
  • Listen for new noises in the attic, a sign animals found a way in

Prevention Tips

10 ways to keep pests out of your Pensacola home

Treatment works best when the house is not inviting pests back in. These simple habits make a big difference.

  • Fix dripping faucets and leaks under sinks. Most pests need water more than food.
  • Keep mulch thin and at least a foot from the walls. Thick mulch hides roaches, ants and termites.
  • Trim trees, palms and shrubs so they do not touch the roof or siding.
  • Store food, pet food and bird seed in sealed bins.
  • Seal gaps around pipes, AC lines, doors and windows.
  • Clean gutters so water drains away from the house.
  • Empty anything that holds water outside at least once a week.
  • Keep firewood and lumber off the ground and away from the house.
  • Check used furniture and luggage for bed bugs before bringing them in.
  • Get a termite inspection every year, even if you see no signs.
